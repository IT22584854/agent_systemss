## 2. Public Health Hierarchy (The "MOH" System)
*Sri Lanka's preventive health sector operates on a geographic grid. Agent must direct users to their "local area" officer.*

### 2.1 Key Roles
* **MOH (Medical Officer of Health):** The doctor in charge of a specific geographic area (e.g., Kaduwela MOH, Dehiwala MOH). Heads the preventive health team.
* **PHM (Public Health Midwife) / "Midwife Nona":** The primary field officer for maternal and child health. Every household belongs to a specific PHM area. *Key for: Pregnancy registration, infant vaccination referrals.*
* **PHI (Public Health Inspector):** The officer responsible for environmental health, communicable disease control (Dengue/COVID), and food safety.
* **PHNS (Public Health Nursing Sister):** Supervises PHMs and manages field clinics.

### 2.2 Finding the Correct Officer
* **Action:** Users should contact their local **Divisional Secretariat** or **MOH Office** to find their specific PHM or PHI.
* **Protocol:** "To register for pregnancy care or vaccinations, you must locate the PHM assigned to your residential address."

## 3. Maternal & Child Health (MCH)

### 3.1 Pregnancy Registration (Antenatal Care)
1.  **Eligibility:** Any pregnant woman resident in the area.
2.  **Process:**
    * Contact the area **PHM** immediately upon positive urine test.
    * PHM registers the couple in the **"Eligible Families Register"**.
    * Two forms are issued: **H 512 A** (Clinic Card - Mother holds) and **H 512 B** (Office record).
    * **Referral:** PHM refers mother to the antenatal clinic at the MOH office or local hospital for dating scan and blood tests (VDRL, Grouping).
    * **Thriposha Program:** Underweight mothers (BMI <18.5) and those with low Hb are eligible for supplementary food (Thriposha).

## 4. Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD)

### 4.1 Healthy Lifestyle Centers (HLC)
* **Function:** Screening centers located in primary hospitals (PMCU/Divisional Hospitals) for people aged 35+.
* **Services:** Blood sugar (FBS), Blood Pressure, BMI calculation, and risk assessment (CVD Risk).
* **Cost:** Free of charge.

### 4.2 Essential NCD Medicines (2022 Revised List)
*Commonly available in government dispensaries (state sector).*

* **Diabetes:** Metformin (500mg), Gliclazide (40mg/80mg), Soluble Insulin, Isophane Insulin.
* **Hypertension:** Amlodipine, Enalapril, Losartan (50mg), Hydrochlorothiazide, Atenolol.
* **Cholesterol:** Atorvastatin (10mg/20mg).
* **Heart Disease:** Aspirin (75mg), Clopidogrel, Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN sublingual).
* **Asthma:** Salbutamol (Inhaler/tablets), Beclometasone (Inhaler), Prednisolone.

## 5. Hospital System Categorization
*Use to determine where to send a patient based on severity.*

1.  **National Hospital of Sri Lanka (NHSL):** Colombo & Kandy. Apex tertiary care. Handles all complex surgeries and trauma.
2.  **Teaching Hospitals (TH):** Attached to medical faculties (e.g., TH Karapitiya, TH Jaffna). Specialized care.
3.  **Provincial/District General Hospitals (PGH/DGH):** Major hospitals with specialists (Consultants) in Medicine, Surgery, Ped, Gyn.
4.  **Base Hospitals (BH Type A/B):** Have basic specialties. Good for initial stabilization.
5.  **Divisional Hospitals (DH) & PMCU:** "Rural hospitals" or "Dispensaries". OPD care only. **No** major surgeries or ICU. Best for minor ailments (fever, wounds).

## 6. Common Disease Protocols

### 6.1 Dengue Fever
* **Triaging:**
    * **Phase 1 (Febrile):** High fever, body ache. *Action: Paracetamol only. No NSAIDs (Brufen/Diclofenac).*
    * **Phase 2 (Critical):** Usually Day 3-5. Drop in platelets, fluid leakage. *Action: Urgent admission if "Warning Signs" appear.*
* **Warning Signs:** Abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, lethargy, liver enlargement.
* **FBC Monitoring:** Mandatory from Day 3 of fever.

### 6.2 Rabies (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis - PEP)
* **Immediate Action:** Wash wound with soap and water for **10-15 minutes**.
* **Treatment:** Go to nearest government hospital immediately.
    * **ARV (Anti-Rabies Vaccine):** Available in most hospitals.
    * **Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG):** Required for severe bites (Category III). Available mostly in Base Hospitals and above.

### 6.3 Leptospirosis (Rat Fever)
* **Risk Group:** Farmers, people exposed to flood water.
* **Prophylaxis:** Doxycycline (200mg) once a week during exposure. Available at MOH offices and PHIs free of charge.
