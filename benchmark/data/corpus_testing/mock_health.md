# Dengue Disease Information

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection common in Sri Lanka.
Symptoms include fever, headache, joint pain, muscle pain, nausea, and skin rash.
Severe dengue can cause bleeding and shock and requires immediate medical attention.

Treatment is mainly supportive. Patients should seek care at government hospitals.
Information provided by the Ministry of Health Sri Lanka.


# Vaccination Services

Vaccination services in Sri Lanka are provided free of charge by government clinics.
Children receive vaccines under the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI).
Vaccination schedules and clinic locations are published by the Ministry of Health.

Pregnant mothers should attend antenatal clinics for recommended vaccines.


# Maternal Health Services

Maternal health services include antenatal care, delivery services, and postnatal care.
Pregnant mothers are advised to attend government maternity clinics.
Emergency obstetric care is available at base hospitals and teaching hospitals.

Information is issued by the Family Health Bureau under the Ministry of Health.


# Emergency Medical Services

In medical emergencies, patients should immediately visit the nearest government hospital.
Emergency treatment is available 24 hours a day at major hospitals.
Ambulance services are coordinated through government emergency response systems.

Delaying treatment can lead to serious complications.
