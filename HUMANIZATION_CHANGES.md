# Research Paper Humanization: Summary of Improvements

## Overview
The research paper on "Multi-Agent Architecture for Multilingual Health Information Retrieval in Low-Resource Contexts" has been systematically humanized while maintaining 8-page limit and avoiding plagiarism concerns.

## Key Improvements Made

### 1. **Introduction - Problem Statement (Sections A & B)**
**Original approach:** Dense bullet-point lists
**New approach:** Flowing narrative paragraphs with human-centered language

- Changed from abstract technical issues to concrete impacts (e.g., "users may delay emergency care, mismedicate, or dangerously self-diagnose")
- Added connective tissue explaining WHY each problem matters
- Used more accessible language while maintaining rigor
- Restructured "Gap in Existing Approaches" to explain practical consequences of each gap

**Example improvement:**
- OLD: "4) Lack of domain-appropriate evaluation frameworks..."
- NEW: "When practitioners substitute embedding cosine similarity as a proxy for ground truth, they introduce a critical methodological error: cosine similarity measures topical overlap, not logical entailment or factual correctness."

### 2. **Proposed Approach & Contributions (Sections C & D)**
**Change:** Merged into unified narrative instead of separate lists
- Better transitions between components
- Explained relationships between intent classification, CRAG, and evaluation
- Reduced structural bulleting; improved prose flow

### 3. **Results Interpretation (All subsections A-C)**
**Philosophy:** Tell the "story" of findings, not just report numbers

#### A. Baseline Comparison
- Added narrative arc: "progression as architectural sophistication increases"
- Contextualized percentages: "58.3% factual accuracy—a concerningly low baseline for medical QA"
- Explained synergistic effects rather than just reporting gains
- Emphasized safety jump (0.72 → 0.97) with clinical motivation

#### B. Multilingual Consistency
- Reframed results around equity principle
- Explained what 2.4% variance means in practical terms
- Connected technical findings to policy implications
- Added context: "uneven quality across languages can translate directly to differential healthcare outcomes"

#### C. Component Ablation
- Changed from mechanical listing to analysis of "where the value comes from"
- Explained specific query patterns that benefit from each component
- Humanized the 18% figure: "specific for those cases where performance initially fails"

### 4. **Discussion Section**
**Transformation:** From technical analysis → practical insights organized by perspective

Restructured into clear takeaways:
- **First:** Intelligent routing is both efficiency AND safety mechanism
- **Second:** Corrective retrieval solves real problems (symptom queries, mixed-language input, temporal context)
- **Third:** Critique prevents harm through layered mechanisms

Added multiple viewpoints:
- **Systems perspective:** Small orchestration > model scaling in safety-critical settings
- **Deployment perspective:** Addresses real-world lack of ground truth
- **Policy perspective:** Multilingual parity as equity requirement (not feature)

### 5. **Key Findings & Conclusion (Sections VIII & X)**
**Before:** Formal restatement of results
**After:** Impactful narrative emphasizing real-world outcomes

- Key findings now emphasize practical insights over metrics
- Conclusion frames system as "practical path for governmental health AI"
- Language emphasizes equity throughout

### 6. **Limitations Section (Section IX)**
**Approach:** Candid, non-defensive acknowledgment of constraints

Changed tone from clinical to conversational while maintaining transparency:
- "worth acknowledging openly"
- Explains practical implications of each limitation
- Distinguishes between measurement limitations and real-world constraints

### 7. **Future Work (Section XI)**
**Improvement:** Explained WHY each item matters

Example:
- OLD: "Real-time integration with public-health surveillance feeds"
- NEW: "Integration with real-time public-health surveillance feeds, allowing the system to remain contextually current as health threats evolve and new guidance emerges. Stale health information exacts costs."
